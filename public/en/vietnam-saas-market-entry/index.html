Why SaaS stalls in Vietnam

Four reasons good SaaS products struggle here

A translated website, not a localised offer

Buyers look for Vietnamese case studies, local references and a team they can meet. Global logos alone rarely earn the first meeting.

The wrong channels

Many foreign teams rely on email and LinkedIn alone. In Vietnam, follow-up and relationship-building often happen on Zalo and at in-person events.

Unqualified sign-ups

Free trials and demo forms attract students, freelancers and very small accounts. Without lead scoring, sales time disappears.

Deals that stall after the demo

B2B decisions involve several stakeholders. Without structured follow-up, deals go quiet after a good first meeting.

Localisation

Localise trust, not just language

A Vietnam-ready SaaS offer usually needs more than translation. Before scaling ad spend, check that you have:

  • A Vietnamese-language website and sales deck written for local buyers, not machine-translated
  • Vietnamese case studies, references or pilot customers you can name
  • Pricing and contracts that fit local expectations (for example VND pricing and local invoicing), confirmed with your finance and legal advisers
  • A Zalo Official Account and a Vietnamese-speaking point of contact for questions and follow-up
  • A presence at local industry events, webinars and communities where relationships are built
  • Founder or country-lead thought leadership on LinkedIn, so buyers know who stands behind the product

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Vietnam a good market for SaaS?

Vietnam has a fast-digitising business base and growing demand for cloud software, but buyers are relationship-driven and expect local proof and support. SaaS companies that localise their offer and build trust on local channels have the best chance of success. Public estimates of the Vietnam SaaS market vary widely depending on how the market is defined.

Do I need a local entity to sell software in Vietnam?

Not always. Many SaaS companies start by selling from overseas or hiring locally through an Employer of Record, then set up an entity once traction justifies it. Requirements depend on your product and contracts, so confirm with a Vietnamese legal adviser.
